Passing a prop firm challenge is the ultimate goal for most modern retail traders. Getting access to $50,000 or $100,000 in trading capital can completely change your life. However, industry statistics show that nearly 90% of traders fail their evaluations. 

The surprising truth? Most traders do not fail because they have a bad trading strategy. They fail because they have absolutely zero understanding of risk management and position sizing.

In this guide, we will break down the exact risk management rules you need to follow to protect your account and secure your funded status.

### The Silent Killer: The Daily Drawdown Limit

Every major prop firm has a daily drawdown limit. Usually, this limit is set at 5% of your starting balance. If you hit this limit at any point during the trading day, your account is instantly breached and you lose your upfront fee.

When traders guess their lot sizes, they are playing Russian roulette with this daily limit. If you take a trade on a highly volatile pair like GBPJPY or an index like NAS100 without calculating the exact pip value, a sudden spike against you can eat up 3% of your account in seconds. Take two bad trades in a row, and your challenge is over.

### The Golden Standard: The 1% Rule

Professional traders do not risk 5% of their account on a single trade. In fact, most professionals risk a maximum of 1% per trade. When you are taking a prop firm challenge, you should be even more conservative.

We highly recommend risking between 0.5% and 1% per trade. 

By risking only 0.5% per trade, you would have to lose 10 trades in a row in a single day to hit your 5% daily drawdown limit. This gives you massive psychological relief. When you know a loss will only cost you a tiny fraction of your account, you can trade without emotion and let your edge play out.

### How to Execute Perfect Position Sizing

Knowing that you want to risk 1% is easy. The hard part is translating that 1% into a specific lot size on MetaTrader or cTrader. 

Because exchange rates fluctuate constantly, the value of a pip changes depending on the currency pair you are trading. If your account is in USD and you are trading EURGBP, you cannot use the standard $10 per pip rule. You have to convert the GBP pip value back into USD.

Doing this manual math while you are trying to enter a fast moving setup is a terrible idea. A single typo on your desktop calculator can result in entering 10 lots instead of 1.0 lots.
