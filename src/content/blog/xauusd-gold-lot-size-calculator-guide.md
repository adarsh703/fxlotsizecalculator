Gold (XAUUSD) is famous in the trading world for its crazy liquidity and massive daily moves. Many traders love it because you can hit your weekly profit target in just one good setup. But if you have traded gold for more than a few days, you know it can completely wipe out your account just as fast.

The biggest reason traders fail their prop firm challenges on XAUUSD is terrible position sizing. They guess their lot size, hope for the best, and get stopped out for a 3% loss instead of the 1% they planned for. 

In this guide, we are going to look at how to calculate your gold lot size perfectly every single time.

### The Problem with Gold Pips

If you are used to trading regular forex pairs like EURUSD, you probably know that a 10 pip move is a decent little trade. On gold, a 10 pip move can happen in the blink of an eye. 

Calculating pips on gold is slightly different than forex. For XAUUSD, a 1 pip move is a $0.10 change in the price of gold. If gold moves from $2400.00 to $2400.10, that is exactly 1 pip. If it moves from $2400.00 to $2401.00, that is a $1 move in the raw price, which equals 10 pips.

### The XAUUSD Lot Size Formula

To figure out your lot size, you need to know three specific numbers:
1. **Your Account Size:** Let's say you have a $100,000 funded account.
2. **Your Risk Percentage:** You want to risk exactly 1%, which is $1,000.
3. **Your Stop Loss in Pips:** Your technical analysis shows your stop loss needs to be 30 pips away (a $3 move in the gold price).

In standard forex, 1 standard lot (1.00) pays $10 per pip. Luckily, most modern brokers treat gold the exact same way. For a standard 100-ounce contract, 1 standard lot of XAUUSD equals $10 per pip.

The manual math looks like this:
*(Risk in Dollars) / (Stop Loss in Pips * Pip Value per Lot) = Lot Size*

**$1,000 / (30 pips * $10) = 3.33 Lots**

If you take a buy position with 3.33 lots and the trade hits your 30 pip stop loss, you will lose exactly $1,000. You are completely protected from taking a massive drawdown hit.
